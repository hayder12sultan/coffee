Our Story

Born in the birthplace of coffee.

Legend says a goat herder named Kaldi noticed his flock dancing with energy after eating red cherries from an unknown tree. That was in Kaffa, Ethiopia — and the world has been awake ever since.

Buna Bet began in 1998 as a three-table room off Bole Road. Three decades later we still roast in small batches over open flame, still grind by hand for the jebena, and still believe that coffee is not a transaction — it is an invitation.

Single origin, always

Traceable lots from twelve Ethiopian regions, bought direct from smallholder farms.

Roasted every morning

Small batches, open flame, cooled by hand. Nothing sits longer than 48 hours.

Served with ceremony

Incense, kolo and three rounds of buna. Sit long. There is no rush here.

The Ritual

Three rounds. One conversation.

The Ethiopian coffee ceremony is not a performance — it is hospitality itself. Green beans are roasted before you, incense fills the room, and the jebena is poured three times. Refusing a round is refusing a friendship.

01

Roast

ቆጠራ · Qotera

Green Yirgacheffe beans are washed and roasted over charcoal at your table. You breathe the first smoke — it is the opening line of the conversation.

02

Grind

ወፍጮ · Wefcho

Still warm, the beans are ground by hand in a wooden mukecha. The room fills with cardamom, clove and the deep sweetness of caramelising sugars.

03

Brew

ጀበና · Jebena

Water and grounds meet in the clay jebena over open flame. It rises three times, is rested, and poured in one unbroken stream from a height.

04

Bless

በረካ · Baraka

Abol, Tona, Baraka — the three rounds. Each one lighter, each one a blessing. Served with kolo and roasted barley, and as much time as you need.
